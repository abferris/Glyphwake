# Session 1 — wade drag by depth + plunge keeps falling

## Goal (user)
1. Wading should not slow to a crawl the instant the feet touch the water. It should slow
   progressively with depth, reaching its slowest at the depth where the head is at/above
   the water — the treading reference.
2. A jump down into water must not slow the vertical movement until either the feet hit an
   underwater bottom (wading) or the body is mostly submerged (treading depth).
3. Wading must still be slower than walking on land.

## Todo status
`docs/future/CURRENT_TODO.md` → "Player Model → Swimming Model": deleted *"When jumping in
water have model slow down when submerged."* as fixed. Added a new open item for the
pre-existing treading/camera latch found while verifying (see "Side finding" below).

## Fixed: wade drag scales with how much of the body is under

**Where:** `Scripts/Player/PlayerController.cs`

**Symptom:** `WadeSpeedFactor = 0.5` was applied as soon as the player was flagged wading,
which happens as soon as the zone overlaps the capsule — so the first toe of water already
cost half the walk speed.

**Cause:** wading was a single boolean × single factor, with no notion of depth.

**Fix:** drag now follows the same feet→head span the treading float and the eye test use
(`_restHeadHeight + _restHeight`, ≈1.45 m for the current rig):
- `WadeMinSpeedFactor = 0.5f` (replaces `WadeSpeedFactor`) — the slowest wading speed, so
  wading is still clearly slower than `MoveSpeed = 5`.
- `WadeDragExponent = 1.5f` — shapes the curve (1 = linear).
- `WadeSubmersion` (public, 0..1) + `UpdateWadeSubmersion()` / `WadeSpeedFactor()`:
  `clamp((WaterSurfaceY - footY) / span, 0, 1)`, speed =
  `lerp(1, WadeMinSpeedFactor, pow(WadeSubmersion, WadeDragExponent))`.

Because the head bone is the same reference `SurfaceHeadHeight = 0` treads on, the drag
bottoms out exactly where swimming takes over, not at an arbitrary wade depth.

## Fixed: a plunge is not a swim

**Where:** `Scripts/Player/PlayerController.cs` (`SimulateSwim`, `EnterWater`)

**Symptom:** jumping/landing in water cut the fall at the waterline, because the surface
branch ran the float spring from the first tick the capsule was in the zone.

**Cause:** the float had no engagement ramp, and `EnterWater` additionally clamped
`_verticalVelocity` to `-MaxSwimVerticalSpeed`, throwing the plunge speed away on contact.

**Fix:**
- `FloatEngageDepth = 0.7f` — the float only blends in once the body is 0.7 of the
  feet→head span under, i.e. the treading depth. Before that the branch uses plain gravity
  and the full `MaxFallSpeed` limit, so the fall keeps building through the waterline.
- Once `displacement >= 0` (at or below the float target) `engage = 1` outright, so the
  tuned treading equilibrium `targetY = WaterSurfaceY + SurfaceHeadHeight - _headLocalY`
  (head on the waterline) is untouched.
- `EnterWater` no longer clamps the fall speed.

## Tint
**`Scripts/Player/CameraController.cs`** — filled in the stub that was already sitting in

swapped onto the camera only while `IsSwimming && IsUnderwater`, so the tint exists below the

- `UnderwaterDepthRange = 14` — how deep it takes to reach full density.
- `UnderwaterSkyAffect = 0.15` — keeps the sky legible instead of drowning it.

`UpdateUnderwaterLook()` runs in `_Process`: swaps the environment on the transition, and each
frame scales `FogDensity` between near and deep by how far the camera is below the surface. That
is the depth cue — the deeper you are, the more the world and the surface above you wash out.

## Surface from below
**`Scenes/World.tscn`** — `Water` gains a child `SurfaceFromBelow`, a second `MeshInstance3D`
sharing the same 9000x9000 `PlaneMesh` and transform, with its own `ShaderMaterial`.

**`Shaders/water_from_below.gdshader`** (new) — a Snell's window:
- `cull_front`, so it only ever draws back faces: invisible from above the water, which is why
  the existing `StandardMaterial3D` surface is untouched and the above-water view is unchanged.
- Inside the 48.6 deg critical cone the surface is thin and you see the sky through it
  (`window_color`, alpha 0.22); past it water becomes a mirror (`mirror_color`, alpha 0.92) and
  the ceiling closes. That lid is what tells you where the surface is and how far under it you
  are — it shrinks and dims as you descend.
- `depth_draw_never` so it never occludes anything behind it, and `unshaded` since the mirror
  is self-lit anyway. Ripples are built in **world** space from the plane's XZ, so they stay put
  as the camera turns.

## Two shader bugs caught before the user ever saw it
1. `facing = clamp(dot(N, VIEW), 0, 1)` was structurally incapable of working. `VIEW` in a
   Godot fragment is always `(0,0,1)` in view space, and the plane's normal points *up*, away
   from an eye underneath — so the dot product is **negative** from below and clamped to 0,
   making `facing = 0` (total grazing angle) everywhere. The window could never open; every
   pixel was mirror. Now `abs(dot(n, normalize(VIEW)))`.
2. The ripple normal was built in view space and multiplied by `VIEW_MATRIX` as if it were a
   world vector, so the ripples slid around as the camera turned. Now the world normal
   `normalize(vec3(dx, 1, dz))` is built from the world XZ and then taken to view space.

## Revision: the "dark circle" was Snell's window, rendered inverted

Reported as a big dark circle overhead that looked like a shadow. It was not a shadow, and the
reason it read like one is the actual bug.

### Ruling out a shadow

Rendered the same underwater up-look seven times, one variable per frame, and diffed the means:

| variant | mean luma | vs base |
| --- | --- | --- |
| base | 120.0 | — |
| sun shadows off | 119.8 | −0.2 |
| water stops casting shadows | 119.9 | −0.1 |
| terrain hidden | 119.9 | −0.1 |

Nothing was casting anything, which matches the physics: the sun sits at 48 deg elevation, so
shadows only fall downward and no object under the water can darken the surface above it.

### What it actually was

Re-rendered with the window forced opaque red and the mirror opaque green to put the disc under
control, then measured the real frame inside those masks. The window covers 68% of the upward
view, the mirror 24%, and the window was the *dimmer* region (luma 117.7 vs 125.1). Un-fogged,
the sky behind the window measured RGB (98, 124, 151) — the `ProceduralSkyMaterial` zenith, the
darkest part of the sky — while the mirror rendered as a flat unshaded RGB (96, 171, 211), about
30 luma brighter than anything actually in the sky.

So the surface was built inside out. Underwater, Snell's window is the *lit* part and the
total-internal-reflection surround is the dark one; this had the mirror brighter than the sky, so
the window read as a dark hole. A dark disc overhead looks like a shadow because a shadow is
visually exactly that.

### Changes
- `mirror_color` was the culprit, and it needed to go dark enough to read as a mirror of the dim
  water below: `(0.30, 0.68, 0.86, 0.92)` → `(0.08, 0.26, 0.40, 0.95)`. Two intermediate values
  were measured and rejected — `(0.12, 0.38, 0.55)` left the level-view ceiling only 6 luma from

- `window_color` → `(0.45, 0.80, 0.92, 0.45)` so the window is unmistakably the lit part.
- `shimmer` 0.08 → 0.16. Under fog the colour contrast is weak, so moving glints are the cue that
  survives.
- Widened the critical-angle ramp to `critical * 0.94`. The old `0.85` smeared the disc edge into
  the surrounding water, and the fog then erased what little edge contrast remained.

Up-look two-tone separation is now 53 luma (p95 133 vs p05 80), up from 41.

is in real water. The lever for a stronger level-view ceiling is fog density, not the shader.

## Revision: the swim clip started before the character got into the water

Reported as the swim animation beginning before the jump into the water had happened.

### Cause

`IsSwimming` is set by zone overlap, not by touching water. `WaterZone._PhysicsProcess` calls
`EnterWater` for every overlapping body, and the zone's `BoxShape3D` is 9000x50x9000 centred at
y 80 with the water surface at y 80, so it spans **y 55..105 — 25 m above the water surface**.
Leaping off a ledge therefore set `IsSwimming` on the way out, and `UpdateAnimation` gated the
swim clip on `IsSwimming && !IsWading`. Airborne over deep water `IsWading` is false (not on the
floor, and the water is not shallow), so the clip switched to `Swim Tread` for the entire
descent, tens of metres above the surface.

`UpdateLean` and the surface-swim head pin had the same trigger, so the body also leaned into the
swim pose and pinned its head to the surface rig while still in the air.

The vertical movement was already correct: the float is gated on `FloatEngageDepth` of
`WadeSubmersion`, so a plunge keeps falling until the body is neck deep. Only the *look* was early.

### Changes
- `PlayerController.AtSwimDepth` added: latched on at `FloatEngageDepth` and released by the same
  `SurfaceHysteresis` that `IsUnderwater` uses, in span fractions rather than metres, so a body
  settling exactly at the threshold cannot chatter between the swim clip and the jump clip.

## Water seen from above now behaves like water

The surface had been a `StandardMaterial3D` with a constant 0.72 alpha: the same opacity looking
straight down into a metre of water as looking out across twenty, and no reflection at all. That
is the one thing real water is not. Replaced with `Shaders/water_from_above.gdshader`.

The model is one physical idea rather than three tuned constants. The shader reads the scene depth,
differences it against the surface to get the thickness of water in front of whatever is under the
surface, divides by the cosine of the view ray to get the distance the ray really travels through
that water, and attenuates Beer-Lambert style. That single path length is what makes grazing views
bury the bottom: a ray going out towards the horizon crosses the same water for a very long way, so
it goes opaque on its own, with no second angle constant to keep in sync. Fresnel on the same angle
adds the reflection, and the opacity is then whatever survives the crossing rather than a constant.

Depth was picked against the world rather than by feel. A raycast sweep put the sea floor anywhere
from 0.5 m to 22 m deep with a median near 12, so `extinction` is 0.25: the shore stays clear, 6 m
is half gone, and past about 15 m there is nothing left to see. All of it gradual, no cutoff.

`tools/probe_above.gd` measures the result by hanging a red marker in the water column and
measuring how much of its red-minus-blue signal survives the surface, which is independent of how
bright the water itself is. Transmission, 1.0 being fully visible:

    depth    85 deg    55 deg    30 deg    15 deg
      1.5 m    0.63      0.61      0.46      0.06
      8.7 m    0.15      0.09     -0.08     -0.46
     16.3 m   -0.04     -0.08     -0.15     -0.90

Monotonic down both axes, which is the whole ask. Two things worth knowing if this is ever tuned
again: 4.7 removed the built-in `DEPTH_TEXTURE` and wants it declared as a `hint_depth_texture`
uniform, and the probe has to render into its own `SubViewport` because the root window only
presents frames when it is actually on screen — a hidden window hands back a stale image and
measures the wrong frame without complaining, which cost a while.

Three assertions in `test_underwater_look.gd` were asserting the old flat look (0.72 alpha,
`cull_mode` on a StandardMaterial3D), so they were replaced rather than worked around: the shader
path, the back face culling, that the three drivers are present, and that `shallow_opacity` stays
well under half so a flat sheet cannot come back. The tint-matches-the-water-colour check now reads
`shallow_color`, so the two are still tied. 29 checks, 0 failures. test_view, test_swim_entry,
test_wade, test_collision, test_spawn and test_stickman all pass, build 0 warnings 0 errors.

## No more flash of no filter on the way through the surface

Reported as a very quick flash of no filter between swimming on the surface and diving. The
underwater tint was swapped onto the camera as a hard boolean on `IsUnderwater`:

    Environment = submerged ? _underwaterEnv : null;

The eye crosses the line, the environment is cleared, and the tint only lands once that swap does,
so there is a frame in there with no filter at all. It is worse than a one-off because the surface
float holds the head on the waterline and keeps nudging the eye across the threshold, so treading
on the spot made it flicker on and off rather than flash once.

Two changes, both in `CameraController`:

- The environment is now **left on the camera** and its fog density scaled by how submerged the eye
  is. Detaching it is what made the transition pop; at zero density it contributes nothing, and the
  duplicate carries the world's own settings, so the above water look is unchanged.
- The strength is driven by a continuous submersion value, `SmoothStep` over eye depth across
  `SubmersionBand`, then `MoveToward` at `SubmersionFadeSpeed`. Easing it over depth alone would
  still pulse with every bob, so it is eased over time as well. Lagging behind the eye is
  invisible; stepping is not.

`MoveToward` rather than an exponential so the fade is symmetric, cannot overshoot, and is still
converging if the eye is moving faster than the fade. At the defaults it takes about a tenth of a
second to go from clear to full tint, which is slow enough to hide a crossing and short enough not
to feel like a bug.

`test_underwater_filter.gd` walks the eye down through the surface and back up and checks the
filter never steps, never reverses while descending, and never leaves the camera without an
environment. The check that actually pins the bug down is that the filter has to produce
*intermediate* values on the way down: the old code could only ever give 0 or 1, so that is
impossible for a switch to satisfy. Verified the test fails against the old implementation — 0
intermediate samples and 8 frames with the environment detached — and passes with 11 intermediate
samples, 0 reversals and 0 detached frames.

The test pushes `FloatEngageDepth` out of reach for its duration, because the float springs the body
back to the waterline and pins the eye on the threshold; without that the eye cannot be walked down
at a slow even pace and the fade gets jumped over instead of sampled. It restores the value after.

`test_underwater_look.gd` had a check that the environment was `null` above water, which was
asserting the mechanism rather than the effect. It now checks `Submersion` is zero instead, with a
comment saying why the environment stays attached, and settles longer because the fade takes about a
tenth of a second to clear. All tests pass, build 0 warnings 0 errors.

## Surface flash, second cause: the swim lift teleported the eye

The first fix removed the hard filter swap and the user still saw a flash of no filter for a
fraction of a moment, every time, in both directions. The filter was not the cause. `probe_surface.gd`
rendered a real dive through a SubViewport each frame and measured how blue the frame actually was,
which showed the rendered tint collapsing from 539 to 31 and recovering over about eight frames.

The cause was in the camera, not the filter. `SwimCameraLift` holds the eye 0.8 m clear of the water
while treading, and the lift target is `SwimCameraLift * (1 - lookDown)`, which reaches zero at 25 deg
of downward pitch. `DiveLookDownDeg` is also 25, so the instant a dive engaged, the old line

    _currentLift = surfaceSwim && targetLift < _currentLift
        ? targetLift
        : Mathf.MoveToward(_currentLift, targetLift, SwimLiftSpeed * dt);

hard assigned the lift instead of easing it. The eye fell 0.8 m in one frame, straight through the
surface and well past the point where the view stops showing blue water and starts showing the
seafloor, and the tint was still on its way up from zero. A stretch of clear, untinted deep water on
every single crossing, in both directions, which is what was being reported. The lift is now always
`MoveToward`, at a cost of 0.8 / SwimLiftSpeed = 0.13 s.

Along the way the fade constants were briefly widened and slowed on the theory that the fade was too
tight, and that made it visibly worse: at `SubmersionBand` 0.55 and `SubmersionFadeSpeed` 2.5 the
rendered tint collapsed for 12 to 13 frames with a minimum of 9 against 31 at the defaults. The tint
has to arrive over the first few centimetres, because that is where the view swaps from the blue
surface to the seafloor. `SubmersionBand` and `SubmersionFadeSpeed` were left at 0.15 and 9.

`test_underwater_filter.gd` grew a seventh check for this, and it is the only one that can catch it.
The lift has to be brought all the way up first, then the view flicked down in a single frame the way
a player flicks the mouse, because easing the pitch down only creeps the target lower and never
trips the snap. Against the old line it reports the eye jumping 0.798 m in one frame, the full
`SwimCameraLift`; with the fix the worst jump is 0.127 m, which is `SwimLiftSpeed` times a frame.
Rendered dive with the fix: lowest blue tint 348 with zero collapsed frames, and the tint reaches
full strength 0.25 s after the eye crosses. The scratch probe was deleted afterwards; the
behaviour it was checking is now covered by the test.

## Surface flash, third cause: the fade had two terms that both started at nothing

With the lift eased the flash was much smaller but still there, and still in both directions. A
`SubmersionBand` over 0.15 m at a fade of 9/s meant the tint took 0.25 s to reach full strength, and
the first part of that reads as no tint at all. The reason it was slow is that the tint was the
product of two terms that both start at zero: a spatial smoothstep over the depth band, and the
`MoveToward` ease over time. Near the surface the spatial term is small, the time term is small, and
they multiply, so the fog was still almost clear a good way under.

`SubmersionBand` is gone, replaced by a latch. `SubmersionOn` (0.05 m under) and `SubmersionOff`
(0.25 m above) with the dead zone between them, and `SubmersionFadeSpeed` raised to 20. The latch is
what makes a short fade safe: a single threshold would let the treading bob drive the tint on and off
every bob, but with a dead zone the eye cannot cross it while treading, because the swim lift holds
the eye 0.64 m clear of the water and the bob is centimetres. The latch engages as soon as the eye is
5 cm under and releases only once it is 25 cm clear above, so the tint is still there while the eye is
breaking the surface and does not vanish before the view has changed over. The depth gradient is
untouched: `t` in the fog density is still `depth / UnderwaterDepthRange`, so shallow water is still
lighter than deep.

Measured with a rendered dive and a rendered surface, both through a SubViewport at 60 fps:

  - frames with the eye under the surface but no tint: 0, was 12 to 13
  - frames rising with the eye under the surface and no tint: 0
  - peak tint while the eye was clear above the water: 0.00, so no treading pulse
  - crossing to full tint: 2 frames, 0.033 s, was 15 frames and 0.25 s
  - lowest rendered bluestness while under: 509 to 541, was down to 9

`SubmersionBand` was removed rather than left at a default, since nothing reads it any more.
`test_underwater_filter.gd` had a hard reference to it in its termination condition and now uses
`SubmersionOn`. All 8 tests pass, build 0 warnings 0 errors.
