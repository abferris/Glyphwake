# Session 1 — plunge holds the upright fall (todo + worklog)

## Goal (user)
Jumping off a surface into the water plays a cannonball-like tuck. It should do an ordinary
falling animation until it hits the water.

## Todo status
`docs/future/CURRENT_TODO.md` → "Player Model → Swimming Model": deleted the "Jumping into the
water plays a cannonball-like pose, to fix later" item's diagnosis, because the transition it
asked for now exists and is measured. Kept, reworded: the dedicated entry/impact clip, the flip
that still happens *after* the handover, and the note that a level look was measured too.

## Fixed: submersion alone was deciding the look

**Where:** `Scripts/Core/LocomotionState.cs`, `Scripts/Player/PlayerController.cs`,
`Scripts/Player/SurfaceSwimPose.cs`

**Symptom:** jumping in swapped `Jumping Up` for `Swim Tread` while the body was still falling
at 6.8 m/s, then the body rolled prone and somersaulted.

**Cause:** the `WaterZone` box spans y 55..105 around a water surface at y 80, so it reaches 25 m
*above* the water, and `LocomotionState.Update` resolved `Mode` from depth alone. Depth runs out
long before speed does — a body dropped from 20 m is 0.6 submerged within two ticks of entering
at 19.98 m/s — so `AtSwimDepth` latched while the fall was still at 6.86 m/s. Two knock-on
effects: `UpdateBodyPitch` keys off `UnderwaterSwimming` and targets `LookPitch - 90 deg`, so a
-35 deg look asked for -125 deg of pitch at `BodyPitchSpeed = 360` deg/s; and
`SurfaceSwimPose.SurfaceActive()` used `IsAtOrAboveSurface`, which includes `EnteringWater`, so
`SubmergeArms` was pulling the hands down to a world height just under the waterline while the
body was still falling towards it from 20 m up.

**Fix:**
- `SubmersionProbe` carries `VerticalVelocity` alongside the rest of the frame's measurements.
- `LocomotionState.IsPlunging` latches on `vy < -PlungeSpeed` and releases at half that, so the
  float spring's overshoot around its equilibrium cannot re-arm it. It is reset in
  `ExitWater()` and on the `!InWater` branch of `Update`.
- `Mode` resolves `IsPlunging` *first*, ahead of `IsUnderwater`/`AtSwimDepth`, mapping it to
  `EnteringWater`. It is the only branch that is a statement about speed rather than depth or
  ground, which is why it goes first.
- `PlungeSpeed = 6` on `PlayerController`, kept live in `UpdateState` alongside the other tuning
  so the tests can retune it at runtime. 6 is measured, not picked: a 20 m drop is still 6.76 m/s
  a tenth of a second after entry, and a deliberate dive tops out at `MaxSwimVerticalSpeed = 4`,
  so the threshold has to sit above 4 or a diving player would be denied its own swimming look.
- `SurfaceSwimPose.SurfaceActive()` now waits on `IsSurfaceSwimming`.

**Verification:** `dotnet build` 0 warnings 0 errors. `tools/probe_cannonball.gd` (20 m drop, level
look): `Jumping Up` held to submersion 1.000 at vy -2.85, handed over at vy -2.52; previously it
handed over at submersion 0.822 while still doing 13.51 m/s. `tools/probe_ledge.gd` (2 m ledge,
camera pitched -35 deg): body pitch 0.00 deg for the whole plunge, `Jumping Up` held through
submersion 1.000, handover at vy 0.00 with the plunge latched for 12 consecutive ticks. The hands
now track the body all the way down instead of being pinned to the waterline. Both probes were
diagnostics and have been deleted; `tools/test_plunge_hold.gd` replaces them.

`tools/test_plunge_hold.gd` (new) drives the low ledge with a downward look, the case the report
came from, and asserts: the swim look never appears while `IsPlunging`, the body pitch stays under
1 deg while plunging, the plunge holds for at least 3 ticks so the window is really covered, the
handover happens no faster than `PlungeSpeed`, and `PlungeSpeed > MaxSwimVerticalSpeed` so a
deliberate dive cannot read as a plunge. `test_swim_entry.gd`, `test_plunge.gd`, `test_wade.gd`
and `test_swim.gd` still pass.

## Two things found and not fixed

**1. `tools/test_stickman.gd` stage 8 still fails, and did before this change.** "the eye spent
191/191 frames underwater while treading", body 0.5 m under, eye 0.8 m under. Verified against a
`git stash` of the working tree: the baseline prints 192/192 with the same three failures, so it
is the pre-existing treading-camera loop already written up in `CURRENT_TODO.md`, not this work.

**2. The body still rolls prone *after* the handover.** With a level look the pitch target is
-90 deg rather than -125, so it still rolls over, just less far, and it takes about a quarter
second at `BodyPitchSpeed`. The trigger is the `IsUnderwater` latch and the eye being under it —
the same lift loop as item 1 — so it belongs in that item rather than being tuned away here.
Pitch damping on splashdown would hide the symptom while the eye stays wrongly submerged.

**One bug of my own, worth recording because it cost the most time to find.** The hysteresis
was written as `IsPlunging ? vy > -PlungeSpeed * 0.5f : vy < -PlungeSpeed`, i.e. release when the
body is *slow*. That released the latch on the very next tick and made the plunge flag alternate
every frame, which reads exactly like a broken float rather than an inverted comparison. It is
worth knowing that Godot headless cannot read a C# struct's members from GDScript — `player.State`
is not exposed — so the latch had to be observed through a pass-through on `PlayerController`
before it could be diagnosed at all.

# Session 2 — swimmer climbs out onto a shallow ledge (todo + worklog)

## Fixed: a swimmer blocked by a ledge stops treading forever
**Where:** `Scripts/Player/PlayerController.cs` (`WadeStepUp`, `WadeStepUpHeight`,
`WadeStepUpProbe`, the shallow-floor branch of `SimulateSwim`),
`Scripts/Core/LocomotionState.cs` (`GroundY`, `IsOverShallowFloor`).

**Symptom:** swimming at a bank with a step in it, the body reached the face of the step and
stayed in `Swim Tread` indefinitely. Not on the floor, not wading, no way onto the shelf.

**Cause:** two separate reasons, and fixing only the first does not reach wading.
1. The float parks the body a fixed depth under the waterline. Measured against the tread
   height of 78.61 in water at 80, so any step whose top is above 78.61 is a wall to the
   body — the shelf the player is trying to reach is exactly the shelf the float cannot see.
2. `AtSwimDepth` is latched with hysteresis around `FloatEngageDepth`, and `IsWading` requires
   the floor contact that the body never has. So even a lift that left the body on the floor
   could strand it in `SurfaceSwimming`, because the swim latch outranks wading and the two
   thresholds (0.7 engaging, 0.92 handing over) put a dead zone between them.

**Fix:** two cooperating pieces.
- `WadeStepUp()` runs right after `MoveAndSlide()`, and only for a `SurfaceSwimming` body that
  is actually against a wall and actually wants to move. It samples the floor 1 m ahead
  (`WadeStepUpProbe`, which has to clear the capsule radius, or it only ever samples the floor
  the body is already standing over) and lifts the body by the rise, provided that rise is
  between 0.01 m and `WadeStepUpHeight`. The bound is the important half: it is why a wall is
  still a wall. No headroom test, so the lip can clip — accepted deliberately, because the body
  is in the water when it happens and a swept test is not worth it yet.
- The surface float disengages when `LocomotionState.IsOverShallowFloor` — wadeable depth with
  reachable floor beneath — and the descent is then clamped to `-MaxSwimVerticalSpeed` instead
  of falling. Without this the lifted body rides at the stand height asymptotically, as an A/B
  with the branch disabled showed: `ground clip=false` on every single frame and the handover
  never happened, because it never actually landed. `GroundY` was added to carry the floor
  height into `PlayerController`, since the state is what knows it.

**Verification:** `tools/test_ledge_wade.gd`, new, two scenarios against a synthetic box.
Scenario 0 builds the box 0.6 m above the measured tread height, so it is climbable and sits in
0.98 m of water; scenario 1 puts it `WadeStepUpHeight + 0.25` up, so it is a wall. It injects
real key events through `Input.parse_input_event` and asserts both directions:
```
climbed at step 271 feet=79.02 mode=2 clip="Walk Forward" wade=true floor=true sub=0.675 131 frames on it
wall run last feet=78.61 mode=4 clip="Swim Tread" wade=false
TEST LEDGE WADE OK
```
The detailed trace shows the whole handover and why the test may not judge it on the first
frame: step 141 is `mode=4` standing on the ledge, step 142 is `mode=2` walking. The state
samples floor contact before the body moves, so the first frame on a new surface is resolved
from the floor the body was over on the way in. An earlier version of the test asserted on that
frame and failed against correct behaviour.

The real-terrain probe (removed, it was diagnostic) went the other way and did not regress:
it still ends `Idle` in `Mode=2` in 0.28 m of water with `Walk Forward` rows in between. The
`tools/probe_*.gd` scripts and their `tmp_*.log` output were deleted once the regression test
covered both directions.

Suite, after the change: `test_ledge_wade`, `test_plunge`, `test_swim_entry`, `test_wade`,
`test_swim`, `test_walkstop`, `test_plunge_hold` all exit 0. `test_stickman` stage 8 still
fails with the same three pre-existing assertions, unchanged by this work.

**Not addressed:** a rise past `WadeStepUpHeight` still refuses, so a 1 m step in the real
terrain is not climbable; the treading camera latch, which is why the eye ends up under.

# Session 3 — a bank too steep to climb no longer sinks the body (todo + worklog)

## Todo status
`docs/future/CURRENT_TODO.md` → "Bugs → Player Model → Swimming Model": extended the line added in
Session 2 with the coupling this introduced. Nothing deleted; the camera latch and the prone flip

## Fixed: swimming into a bank too steep to walk up pulled the body under the water
**Where:** `Scripts/Player/PlayerController.cs` (`SampleWadeStepUpRise`, `_wadeStepUpRise`,
`WadeStepUp`, the shallow-floor branch of `SimulateSwim`).

**Symptom:** swimming at a bank too steep to climb, the model sank below the surface instead of
treading on it — down out of `Swim Tread` into `Swimming Loop`, about half a metre under the
float, held there rather than bobbing back.

**Cause:** the settle added in Session 2 stands the surface float down over any floor inside
`WadeDepth`, on the assumption there is somewhere to hand over to. A bank too steep to climb is
shallow floor with nothing behind it: `IsOverShallowFloor` is true, the float disengages, and the
body drops to a floor it cannot stand on. Worse, the drop feeds itself. Sliding down the slope
takes the floor away underneath the body until the water there is deeper than `WadeDepth`, at
which point the settle switches off again and the float catches it — so the body settles into a
steady state a half metre low with the settle chattering on and off against the threshold, and
the treading pose never comes back. `WadeStepUp` could not rescue it either: it only runs in
`SurfaceSwimming`, and once the body went under, `IsUnderwater` outranks it and the lift is off.

The fix is not a bigger threshold but making the two decisions answer the same question. `WadeStepUp`
already asked it — is the floor a stride ahead within the step-up budget — and the settle was
asking a different one — is there shallow floor underneath — so they could disagree, and the
settle could stand the float down for a step the lift had already refused. The rise is now
sampled once per frame by `SampleWadeStepUpRise`, read by the lift as before and by the settle as
a precondition: `overShallowFloor` also requires `_wadeStepUpRise <= WadeStepUpHeight`. There is
now no configuration where the settle hands over to a step the lift will not take.

**Verification:** `tools/test_swim_slope.gd`, new. A ramp is built with its top face centred
under the body's start point, so the body is standing on the slope from the first tick, and the
scenario angles are derived from the budget rather than authored: steeper than
`WadeStepUpHeight / WadeStepUpProbe` by 14 deg, and shallower by 4, so the test straddles the
line that actually decides it. The ramp's profile is read back off the collision that was built
rather than trusted, and the foot of it is placed off the body's measured tread height so the
slope runs out from under the swimmer instead of appearing inside the capsule — starting short of
the near edge instead leaves the body balanced on the edge over open seabed, which reads as a
pass here without ever engaging the settle.
```
steep: lowest feet=78.43 tread=78.42 underwater=0 waded=0
climbable: highest feet=82.81 tread=78.6 waded=201
TEST SWIM SLOPE OK
```
The steep run holds the float equilibrium exactly and never goes under; the climbable run still
stands and walks the slope, 201 frames of it, so the ledge fix is not what was traded away. With
the new precondition removed and nothing else changed the steep run sags to 77.96 against a tread
height of 78.42 and the test fails, which is the shape of the original report.

The judgement window is the hold only. The tail after the key is released is run but not
recorded: where the body drifts to once it is let go says nothing about being swum at a bank, and
recording it renumbers the steps so the two phases collide.

Suite: `test_swim_slope`, `test_ledge_wade`, `test_plunge`, `test_swim_entry`, `test_wade`,
`test_swim`, `test_walkstop`, `test_plunge_hold` all exit 0. `test_stickman` still fails on the
same three assertions, unchanged.

**Not addressed:** `WadeStepUpHeight` is now both the lift budget and the reachability test, so
retuning it moves two things at once — noted in the todo. Nothing looks at how a body should
behave sliding sideways along a bank it cannot climb.

# Session 4 — uphill walking: full speed to 35 deg, slower but walkable to 60 deg (todo + worklog)

## Todo status
`docs/future/CURRENT_TODO.md` → "Bugs → Land Movement": added the unreviewed half of the
slope bands and the contact-stick change to the existing slip-mechanic item, which stays
open. The reason for each is below.

## Added: speed-banded uphill walking
**Where:** `Scripts/Player/PlayerController.cs`
**Request:** normal speed up to 35 degrees, walkable but slower up to 60, not walkable past.

Three new exports after the wade parameters: `SlopeMaxAngleDeg` (60),
`SlopeFullSpeedDeg` (35), `SlopeMinSpeedFactor` (0.45). `_Ready` sets
`FloorMaxAngle = Mathf.DegToRad(SlopeMaxAngleDeg)` because the scene had never set it and
was living on Godot's 45 degree default — which is why a 50 degree bank could not be walked
at all before this. `SlopeSpeedFactor(direction)` reads `GetFloorNormal()`, turns it into a
slope angle, returns 1 up to `SlopeFullSpeedDeg` and lerps down to `SlopeMinSpeedFactor` at
`SlopeMaxAngleDeg`; it multiplies into the existing speed line in `SimulateGround`.

The factor only bites **uphill**: it takes the floor normal's horizontal part as the fall line
and leaves the factor at 1 when the movement points down it. Nothing was asked for downhill,
and charging for it would have been a silent extra change to how the game already feels.

## Fixed: the contact stick was cancelling the climb
**Symptom:** the first test run measured 57.5 deg at the right speed (2.51 m/s) but
`climbed=0.0 m` — the body stood on the face and slid instead of walking up, while 47.5 deg
climbed fine.

**Cause:** the ground stick. `SimulateGround` set `_verticalVelocity = -2f` on every grounded
frame to hold the body onto bumps, and that is a real velocity, so on a steep face its share
along the floor is pure cancellation: 2 m/s down a 60 degree face is 1.73 m/s downhill, against
a character only asked for 2.25 uphill. The climb and the stick were within 0.5 m/s of each
other, so 50 deg up worked and 57.5 deg did not.

**Fix:** the stick is now conditional on there being no input —
`_verticalVelocity = direction.LengthSquared() > 0.0001f ? 0f : -2f`. The stick exists to stop
a *standing* body drifting off a bump; when there is movement input the input already presses
the body into the floor, so the stick was doing double duty and losing.

**Verification:** `dotnet build` clean. `tools/test_slope_speed.gd` (new) builds a ramp on a
dry spot found by raycasting the world, drops the body on the face and holds one move key:

| angle | speed | climbed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 4.70 m/s | flat |
| 35 | 4.70 m/s | 2.98 m |
| 47.5 | 3.53 m/s | 2.32 m |
| 57.5 | 2.51 m/s | 1.43 m |
| 66 | 0.00 m/s | blocked, slid back down |

The test measures the face back off the collision rather than trusting the authored angle, one
tick after the shape is added (a shape is not in the space yet on the tick it is created), so
the printed profile confirms the body stood on the angle it was asked about.

## Adjusted: the swim slope test's steep bank
`tools/test_swim_slope.gd` derived its two slopes from the movement limits, and raising
`FloorMaxAngle` to 60 meant the old "too steep to stand on" bank at ~51 deg was now inside the
walkable range — so it was re-derived as `SlopeMaxAngleDeg + 8`, and at 68 deg the bank dipped
0.27 m against the surface float, just over the test's 0.25 m tolerance.

That was not the stick (reverting the stick gave the same 0.27 m) and it is not submersion —
`underwater=0` and `waded=0` both hold; it is a body pressed against a near-vertical face being
nudged down its tangent. Rather than widen the tolerance for an arbitrarily far-past cliff, the
bank is now `SlopeMaxAngleDeg + 2` = 62 deg. That measures the boundary that actually matters —
one step past the angle the character can walk, which is the line the user asked for — and it
passes at 0.23 m.

**Not addressed:** the 35 deg break and the 0.45 floor were picked to make the bands obvious,
not measured against real terrain; how 45–60 reads in play is worth a pass. Walking down a
steep slope now leans on `floor_snap_length` for contact, which nothing exercises yet.
