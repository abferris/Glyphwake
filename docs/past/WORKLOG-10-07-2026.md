## Fixed: the head dips down while a jump clip plays

**Where:** `Scripts/Player/Animation/Movement/AirbornePose.cs` (new),
`Scripts/Core/AnimationIds.cs` (`IsJumpClip`), `Scenes/Player.tscn` (the modifier node),
`tools/probe_jump_head.gd` (new).

**Symptom (user):** when the model jumps, the head dips down; it should stay upright.

**Cause:** measured off the source clip JSON (`Assets/PolyOne/Free Stickman/Animation/
stickman_clips.json`, the "Jumping Up" the jump clips are built from): the spine pitches
forward ~18° at the coil (t≈0.33) and ~28-40° at the descent tuck (t≈0.80), while the
authored Neck counter only reaches ~6° and is gone by the tuck — so the head rides the
spine down. The clip is authority for the tuck; the head going with it is the bug.

**Fix:** `AirbornePose`, a `SkeletonModifier3D` under `Visual/Skeleton3D` mirroring the
swim's `SurfaceSwimPose` wiring. While the locomotion state is a jump clip (`AnimationIds.
IsJumpClip`: the bare "Jumping Up" — standing jumps and ledge falls — or "Jump Forward L/R"),
it takes every tilt from vertical out of the head bone's global pose and keeps the facing it
already has, so the body coils and the head does not — the read the baked body lean's design
comment asks for. The head's position is left where the clip put it, and the correction
holds for `ReleaseTime` (0.3, exported) after the state leaves the jump clip, so the travel
crossfade — which blends through the clip's tucked end pose — never shows the tuck in the
head; the handover pop is the landed pose's own few-degree tilt.

Two findings on the way, both verified by the probe:

1. **The modifier pass discards the head write.** Applying the correction from
   `_ProcessModificationWithDelta` ran every frame (probe-proven) and changed nothing — a
   `SetBoneGlobalPose` issued inside the SkeletonModifier3D pass is discarded for bones the
   clips animate, exactly `SurfaceSwimPose`'s own comment about the shoulders. The
   correction moved to `_Process` with `ProcessPriority = 100`, where the swim's arm clamp
   lives, and it holds. Note: the swim's own head level still sits in the modifier pass and
   is therefore almost certainly discarded too — invisible today only because the
   synthesized tread clip is upright on its own; the tests assert the head bone's *height*
   (the float pivot), never its level.
2. **Standalone scripts must use `-s` now.** `project.godot` gained
   `run/main_scene="res://Scenes/World.tscn"`, and a script passed as the last positional
   argument no longer runs — Godot runs the main scene instead and the headless process
   sits there until killed (every probe call in the godot-console skill's positional form
   hangs; `run_tests.sh` already uses `--script` and was never affected). Verified:
   positional → timeout with zero script prints; `-s res://tools/tmp_tick_test.gd` →
   "init ran", exit 0.

**Verification:** probe `tools/probe_jump_head.gd` headless (`-s`, exit 0): the head's tilt
from vertical while a jump clip plays is **0.00°** on a neutral ground jump and **0.00°** on
a W-held jump (the runtime lean pitches the visual; the head stays bolt upright — the

## What changed and why

Stamina already existed as a pool with its own max and 10/s regen, its `CostResource`
value, a `StatKey` pair and the dial's white bar — with no spender anywhere (the only two

- **`Scripts/Player/Data/PlayerTechniques.cs`** — `TryAirJump` no longer hard-codes the
  mana literals. It walks `Technique.Costs` and pays every entry out of the resource the
  entry declares, checking *all* entries before paying *any*, so the atomic contract
  (-1 = nothing happened and nothing was spent) holds across a mixed set of costs. The
  double jump then pays whatever its `.tres` says, and the next technique (air dash) pays
  stamina by declaring it — no code change.
- **`Resources/Glyphs/Techniques/DoubleJump.tres`** — the cost entry now carries
  `Resource = 2` (`CostResource.Stamina`; enums serialize as ints, the way `StatKey` does
  in the same file), the sub-resource id is `GlyphCost_stamina`, and the description says
  "spend less stamina".
- Comments updated where they named the pool: `TechniqueGlyphData.Costs`, the
  `TryAirJump` call-site comment in `PlayerController.cs` (~line 550). The active-cast
  comment (~line 656) still says mana and is correct — actives are unchanged.
- **Docs:** `GlyphDecisions.md` (techniques consume the resource their entry declares;
  double jump needs stamina; "spend less stamina"), `Scripts.md` (`PlayerResources` —
  stamina now has a spender), `PROJECT_TREE.md` (the `.tres` line now reads Stamina).
- **Probes:** `probe_airjump_arc.gd` prints `stamina=` per tick, `probe_techniques.gd`

## Fixed: the shoulder girdle hunches up under the upright head during jumps

**Where:** `Scripts/Player/Animation/Movement/AirbornePose.cs` (the pose holder grows a
second correction), `Scripts/Core/PlayerRig.cs` (`Spine1BoneName`/`Spine2BoneName`),
`tools/probe_jump_head.gd` (the pose line prints the spine pitches and the shoulder gap).

**Symptom (user):** with the head stable, the shoulders come up too high during the jump —
a hunchback.

**Cause (measured in-engine, probe):** the jump clips curl the upper back hard — Spine1
8-70°, Spine2 15-104° through the arc (the directional jump's clip freezes at its end pose,
Spine2 104°, for the last ~0.3 s of a high jump's arc) — and the shoulder girdle rides the

**Fix:** `AirbornePose` now takes `UpperBackDamp` (0.8, exported) of the curl away from

child of Spine1, so the second counter reads the pose the first already corrected and the
two compose — the girdle comes back down together. The tilt is measured in skeleton space
(the pose basis excludes the visual's own lean), so the damp takes the clip's curl and
leaves the designed 12° direction tilt alone. Positions are untouched; the lower back keeps
its coil, so the jump still reads as a crouch and extend. Both corrections share the
release hold (`ReleaseTime`), so the crossfade never shows the tucked pose.

**Verification:** probe headless (`-s`, exit 0): Spine1/Spine2 now 2-17° (neutral) and

## Why (user): "it still looks like a shrug … there is no hands up, and the punching arm does not

angles, shoulders a touch up rather than thrust forward; the punching forearm's hand drives
