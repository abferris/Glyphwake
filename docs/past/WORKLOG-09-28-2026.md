# Worklog 2026-09-28

## The underwater flash was not the fade

The 09-27 entry closed out the surface flash as fixed, on the strength of a rendered dive probe
that reported no untinted frames under the surface and a crossing to full tint in 2 frames. That
turned out to be wrong, and the probe was measuring the wrong thing. The user still saw untinted
frames, view dependently: clean looking straight down, still visible diving while looking forward
and down.

Ruled out along the way, so they are not worth trying again:

- near plane clipping, `near = 0.05` and `near = 0.001` behave identically
- the underwater ceiling leaking into the above-water view, it is `cull_front`
- the environment detaching from the camera
- the swim lift teleporting, fixed and regression tested on 09-27

The fade rate was the wrong suspect, and proving that was the useful part. `SubmersionFadeSpeed`
was swept at 20, 45, 80 and 140/s and the worst forward-and-down frame barely moved. Driving it
to 60/s puts the tint at 1.0 on the first frame past the threshold, density jumping 0.000 to
0.030 with no ramp at all, and changes the forward-and-down view by about nothing. So 20/s stays
and the gradual-fade check keeps passing. Raising it to 60 also breaks that check, which is
correct: the test was pinning the thing the user had reported as a bug.

The real cause was that the water haze was far too weak, not the fade and not the world's fog.

`UnderwaterFogNear` was 0.03 and `UnderwaterFogDeep` 0.16. Looking forward and down the tint barely
registered, which is what read as untinted. That view is the hard case for a reason: the ceiling in
`water_from_below.gdshader` is edge on there and contributes nothing, so the haze is the only thing
left that can read as underwater. Looking straight down the ceiling carries it and the haze barely
matters, which is exactly why that direction always looked clean.

Raised to 0.9 and 2.2, so the haze is strong enough to carry the tint on its own. The density is
still `full * _submersion`.

### The wrong turn, and why the world's fog must stay cancelled

Halfway through this I read the world's 0.45 aerial fog being overwritten with zero above the
surface as a second bug, on the strength of a comment in the code claiming "the duplicate carries
the world's own settings, so the above water look is unchanged". The comment is inaccurate, but the
behaviour is deliberate and correct: the camera owns its environment permanently, and carrying it at
zero density is the only reason the world's 0.45 blue aerial fog never shows up. I rebased the
density onto the world's value with `Lerp(_worldFogDensity, full, _submersion)`, which raised the
above-water density from 0 to 0.45 and put a blue haze over everything above the waterline.

Caught by eye, not by a test, which is the honest reason it is written down here: the rendered pixel
measurement is unusable on this code for the traps listed at the end of this entry, and no existing
test asserted anything about the density above the waterline. Reverted to `full * _submersion`.

`test_underwater_filter.gd` now pins both halves so this cannot be reintroduced from either
direction: exactly 0 above the waterline with the filter cleared, and above 0.5 under it. 7 checks
to 9. `UnderwaterFogDensity` on CameraController is what the tests read.

Third trap, found when starting the screen-space water pass: `hint_depth_texture` is **not supported
in `canvas_item` shaders** in 4.7, so the usual `BackBufferCopy` + full-rect `ColorRect` full-screen
post-process cannot read scene depth. Measured it: the reconstructed depth came back constant at
200.00 across 3600 samples, which is what a uniform that never binds looks like. A screen-space water
pass therefore has to be a `CompositorEffect` with a compute shader, not a 2D overlay. The existing
spatial shaders reading `DEPTH_TEXTURE` prove depth is available to 3D shaders; it is the 2D path
that is closed.

The rendered pixel half of the measurement is not trustworthy and is worth recording so it is not
retried. Reading `Camera3D.Environment` from a script gets the *world's* environment back, not the
camera's own, so a mirror camera handed that renders the above-water look and reports the tint as
doing nothing. Handing over the real object via an exposed property still did not work, and writing
a C# `[Export]` float from GDScript reads back as having taken while the C# field never saw it, so
the tint cannot be A/B'd by overriding those at runtime. The fix is pinned in C# and by the two
tests instead; the remaining check is a human looking at it, which no probe substitutes for.

Also fixed a compile error on the way: line 116 read `UnderwaterFogAffect`, which does not exist.
The field is `UnderwaterSkyAffect`. It was not reported as a build failure, which is worth being
suspicious of.

## Pre-existing, not from this work

`tools/test_stickman.gd` stage 8 fails, deterministically, on treading water: body 0.5 m under the
surface, eye 0.8 m under, underwater on every frame. 0.8 m is exactly `SwimCameraLift`, and there
is a feedback loop. The lift target is gated on
`surfaceSwim = IsSwimming && !IsUnderwater && !IsWading`, so the moment the eye dips under, the
lift that holds the eye clear switches off and eases to zero, the eye drops a further 0.8 m, and
being further under keeps it under.

This is not caused by the fog work. It reproduces identically with the old 0.03/0.16 values and
the new 0.9/2.2 values, verified by reverting just those two numbers and re-running.

It is the same shape of problem the tint latch already solves with a dead zone between
`SubmersionOn` and `SubmersionOff`: the lift has no equivalent hysteresis. Not started, since it
is a separate bug from the flash.

# Session 2 — input map decisions (ControlsDecisions + todo)

Documentation only, no code touched. Everything written down here is marked **tentative** —
these are defaults chosen so the game is playable, and the right-click split in particular is
the number most likely to change once it is played.

**Where:** `docs/design/ControlsDecisions.md`, `docs/future/CURRENT_TODO.md`

## What was decided

Right click was a `???` placeholder in `ControlsDecisions.md` ("Right click will ???"). It is
now the world-interaction verb: tap to interact with whatever the crosshair is on.
Context-sensitive under one verb — talk, loot, pick up, open, quest hand-in. Crosshair on
nothing does nothing, with no nearest-object fallback.

**Correction to the doc's own claim.** The status block said hold-right-click-to-look was
"already shipped". It is not — it was in the codebase once and has since been removed.
`CameraController.cs:258` gates look on `Input.MouseMode == Captured` and nothing else, so
look is always-on while the cursor is captured, and grepping `Scripts/` for `MouseButton.Right`
/ `RightButton` finds nothing. The doc header and the input map table now say so. This matters
beyond bookkeeping: **RMB is genuinely unclaimed**, so the interact decision is not fighting an
existing binding.

The reasoning is that the mouse is locked and hidden, so interaction reuses the same raycast
that already aims glyphs: LMB casts at what you're looking at, RMB interacts with it. One aim
model, two verbs. Interact and *target* are explicitly kept separate — targeting is sticky and
drives threat, nameplates and group content, and binding it to RMB would mean interacting
silently changes who is marked.

**The secondary glyph cast is not on right click.** The earlier suggestion in this session was
a tap/hold split where the tap fired the secondary form. That is dead: the tap is spent on
interact, and two taps on one button does not disambiguate. Secondary casts get their own
binding (second hotbar set, or a modifier on LMB).

**Cast preview is on hold F**, middle mouse as the fallback default. Release cancels;
F + LMB together commits while still previewing, which is what totem placement mid-fight
needs. Projectiles show the flight path; location glyphs show the effect circle inside the
cast-range circle — the same two decals the location-glyph hold already specced, so there is
one visual dialect for "where does this land" rather than two.

Rejected alternatives, recorded so they are not re-proposed: **overloading RMB's hold** (it is
already the look key, and gating it on "nothing interactable" means losing the ability to look
around near NPCs — most of the time in a town) and **hold Ctrl** (pinky fatigue through
combat, turns every key into a combo, and Ctrl is the genre's slot for target/nameplate info).

## Remapping is a requirement, not tentative

Stated as such in the doc: every action is remappable, including the mouse buttons, the
RMB tap/hold split, and the preview key. Bound by action rather than key so gameplay code
cannot assume a physical key, with conflict detection and a reset-to-defaults. The RMB
tap/hold split is two remappable actions sharing one button, so the hold threshold travels
with the action.

## Todo status

`docs/future/CURRENT_TODO.md` → "Mechanics to create": added an **input map** block
(rebinding, RMB interact/look, hold-F preview, interact-vs-target split) as new work. It was
not on the list before; the doc now says the input map is a todo, so it is listed.

## Left open

- Whether a long press on an NPC also interacts with it — press-on-press versus
  release-under-threshold. Most MMO-likes take a long press as a valid interact, leaning that
  way with a short (~120 ms) hold threshold, but it is not settled.
- Whether hold-to-look is still wanted at all now that interact owns the RMB tap. Look is
  always-on and works, so a hold-RMB-look would be a *precision* mode, and it may well be
  redundant with the F-hold preview, which already trains the player to hold a key while
  aiming. Documented as an open question rather than assumed away.

# Session 3 — water tint compositor: depth reconstruction proven (todo + worklog)

## Todo status
`docs/future/CURRENT_TODO.md` → added a "Water Tint Compositor" section. The reconstruction and
tint work is recorded as **Verified**; wiring, the Environment fog handover, and a human look are
recorded as still to do. Nothing was deleted — no existing todo item was closed by this session.

## The bug: unprojecting depth by inverting the view-projection matrix

**Where:** `Scripts/World/WaterTintEffect.gd`, `Shaders/water_tint.glsl`

**Symptom:** every sample reconstructed to roughly the same place, near the camera, regardless of
which pixel was read. A frame came out that looked like plausible water with the geometry in the
wrong place, which is the worst kind of failure to diagnose by eye.

**Cause:** two separate things, and the second was only findable because of the first.

1. Godot 4.7 renders **reverse-Z**: the depth buffer holds 1 at the near plane and 0 at the far
   plane. Reading it the conventional way maps everything to the near plane, so every pixel
   reconstructs as "just in front of the camera" — a self-consistent picture, entirely wrong.
   The depth-to-distance step is `dist = n*f / (n + d*(f - n))`, which gives `n` at `d = 1` and `f`
   at `d = 0`.

2. Inverting the view-projection matrix is numerically hopeless at this camera's depth range. With
   `near = 0.05` and `far = 4000` the inverse matrix holds entries of order 1e4, which then get
   differenced against world coordinates of order 1e3. Float32 has ~7 significant digits, so the
   difference of two numbers around 1e7 retains almost nothing of the part that matters. The
   reconstruction stopped depending on where the pixel was at all.

   This is why it survived so long: it is not a sign error or a transpose, both of which produce
   obviously broken output. Gauss-Jordan inversion itself was verified correct in isolation
   (`M * inverse(M) = I` to float precision), the camera-derived matrix was verified correct by hand,
   and `get_view_projection(0)` was verified to belong to a different view than expected. All three
   were fine. Only measuring `got` per probe direction showed the tell: `got` was near-identical
   across all four directions while `want` varied correctly.

**Fix:** don't build the matrix at all. The camera is pushed as a basis plus the projection's scale
factors (`near`, `far`, `tan(fov/2)`, `tan(fov/2)*aspect`), and the shader does a depth-to-distance
division followed by a basis rotation. Two well-conditioned steps instead of one badly-conditioned
one, and cheaper. `RenderSceneData.get_view_projection()` is no longer used.

## The probe was wrong too, twice

Worth recording, because both produced convincing false failures that pointed at the shader.

**A neighbouring surface, not a reconstruction error.** Samples aimed at the underwater plate came
back ~30 m high. The plates were 60 m across at 28/42/56 m, so their footprints overlapped in XZ,
and the sample was measuring whichever plate was in front. The occlusion check only tested the
sampled plate against *itself* and never its neighbours. Fixed by making the three footprints
provably disjoint, by testing every plate for each ray, and — the part that actually mattered — by
having the shader encode world X and Z as well as height, so "correct point, wrong height" is
distinguishable from "right height, wrong piece of geometry".

**The real world was occluding the reference.** Even with disjoint plates, the underwater plate still
read ~30 m high, reconstructing to a point ~29 m from the camera. That is the actual shoreline in
`World.tscn`. The probe hid real geometry by searching for `VisualInstance3D` — and **Terrain3D is
not a `VisualInstance3D`**, so the shoreline kept drawing and silently decided the depth of every
underwater sample. The probe now hides by property presence, keeping only lights and cameras (the
plates would render black without the lights, and another camera would take the viewport). A
reference measurement cannot share its scene with the thing it is measuring.

Two smaller probe bugs, both of which had been reported as shader failures: the X and Z decode
inverted the shader's encoding with a spurious factor of 2, and the debug gradient was read out of
the alpha channel, which is not sRGB-encoded. Height was encoded without the half-offset that X and Z
use, which is why height recovered correctly and the other two did not.

## Verified

`tools/probe_effect.gd`, three reference plates straddling the waterline:

- reconstruction: **12 of 12 within 0.5 m, worst error 0.11 m**, every sample on its intended plate
- tint, same samples, real pass: submerged pixels land on exactly `(0.022, 0.078, 0.112)` — the deep
  water colour, `UnderwaterTint * 0.25` — and above-water pixels are untouched
- `WaterTintEffect` prints its own CPU round-trip error on the first frame: `0.000000 m`
- `tools/test_underwater_filter.gd` 9 checks 0 failures; `tools/test_underwater_look.gd` 29 checks 0
  failures

## Also fixed: `UnderwaterFogNear`/`Deep` had been reverted to 0.06/0.19

**Where:** `Scripts/Player/CameraController.cs:27-28`

`test_underwater_filter.gd` failed with "lowest 0.061 at full tint" against a required `> 0.5`. The
file held 0.06/0.19 while both the test and the previous session's notes said the accepted values are
0.9/2.2. Restored, and the stale comment at line 316 that referred to the intermediate pair
("the old 0.03 to 0.16") now names the pair that was actually wrong.

The C# change needed a rebuild to take effect — `dotnet build Glyphwake.csproj` — because a headless
`-s` script run loads the prebuilt assembly from `.godot/mono/temp/bin/Debug/` and does not
recompile. Worth remembering: a C# edit that appears to do nothing under a headless test run is a
stale assembly, not a failed hypothesis.

## Known-unresolved, deliberately

The Environment fog handover. The compositor decides the tint per pixel, but the camera still drives
its own duplicated environment's `FogDensity`, and that fog is baked into every pixel *before* the
compositor runs. Above water the compositor can add tint on the submerged half but cannot remove the
camera fog from the dry half. The options and a recommended order are written up in the todo; this
is a design call, not something to settle by default.

# Session 4 — water look ownership decision recorded (todo only)

## Todo status
`docs/future/CURRENT_TODO.md` → new top-level `## Water Look Ownership` section, added above
`## Bugs` at the user's request. No code changed in this session. Discussion only; the section
records a design question and the reasoning, and commits to nothing.

## The question
Whether the underwater colour should come from the `ZoneShape` child of `WaterZone` — as both a
reference for *where* the effect applies and as the source of the colour — while near/deep fog
density stays in C#.

## What the investigation turned up
`ZoneShape` is a `CollisionShape3D` (`Scenes/World.tscn:115`), not a scripted node.

**The extent is genuinely triple-defined and only synchronised by hand:**
- `WaterSurfaceY = 80.0` — float on the zone, `Scripts/World/WaterZone.cs:6`, `Scenes/World.tscn:113`
- XZ footprint — the `ZoneShape` `CollisionShape3D`, `Scenes/World.tscn:115`
- the surface actually rendered — separate 9000x9000 `PlaneMesh` on the `Water` mesh, `Scenes/World.tscn:101`

`tools/build_world.gd:93` sets `WaterSurfaceY` to 55.0 while the scene holds 80.0, so drift is
already possible depending on whether that tool is still live — not verified either way.

**The colour half was already tried and failed for a narrower reason than it appears.**
`docs/past/WORKLOG-09-27-2026.md:293` records the first pass sourcing the tint from the zone's
`debug_color` `(0, 0.6, 0.698)`, which "read as pale aqua and did not look like the same water you
were looking at from above" and was moved onto the surface albedo `(0.09, 0.31, 0.45)`. `debug_color`
is an editor gizmo value that happened to hold a leftover, not an authored look value. So the
recorded rejection is evidence against the *field*, not against the user's architecture.

**A `CollisionShape3D` cannot hold an `[Export] Color`** without attaching a script to a physics
shape to hold art data, which is a smell regardless of the source-of-truth question.

## The conclusion recorded
Split ownership by concern rather than moving everything to the zone:
- **where** → `WaterZone` + `ZoneShape`, with the mesh deriving from it so trigger and effect cannot desync
- **colour** → the `Water` `ShaderMaterial`, or a `.tres` shared by the material and the effect; the
  fix is to delete `CameraController`'s private copy, not to re-host the value
- **density** → stays in C#, because it is a property of the view rather than the body, and is
  per-body-extensible for free via the array `WaterTintEffect.gd` already carries

Practical target is smaller than a restructure: `CameraController` keeps only the density curve and
loses `UnderwaterTint`. Sequenced ahead of wiring the compositor, since wiring it first would bake
the single-camera-tint model into `Scenes/World.tscn`.

## Not verified
Whether `tools/build_world.gd` is still in use, and whether the 55.0/80.0 difference is real drift
or a stale tool. No human eye has looked at a half-submerged frame.

# Session 5 — `CameraController` split proposal filed as revisit-only (todo)

## Todo status
`docs/future/CURRENT_TODO.md` → new `## Revisit before changing: splitting CameraController.cs`
appended at the end of the file, below `# Next Work To Do`. Explicitly marked do-not-start-as-a-
refactor. No code changed. Discussion only.

## The proposal
Split `Scripts/Player/CameraController.cs` into three files — water, land, debug third-person
camera — as C# `partial` classes compiled into one type.

## What the file actually contains
367 lines. Exports are mouse look and pitch limits, FOV, mouse capture, swim camera lift, the
underwater look block (21-36), submersion thresholds (46-57), and the view mode block (61-65).
Two of the three proposed files have nothing behind them:

- **No land section exists.** Land movement is in `PlayerController.cs`.
- **The debug camera is already a separate file**, `Scripts/Player/DebugChaseCamera.cs` (2 KB, own
  `.uid`), and is already marked for deletion before shipping in the Camera F1/F3 item.

## Why `partial` is the wrong tool
It splits a *type*, not a node. Nothing is imported or merged — the compiler emits one class and the
IDE presents it as one class in regions. Every piece retains access to every private field, so it
moves where code is read without changing dependencies, and it remains invisible in the scene tree.
Decisive: the head camera and the F3 chase camera both render the water (see the compositor wiring
item), and a `partial class CameraController` binds the look to one camera node, so the other cannot
have it without duplicating the partial or the code. A child `Node` can serve both.

## The part that is not cleanly separable
The water block is the only section with an argument behind it — about 130 of 367 lines (exports
21-57, fields 76-78, environment duplicate in `_Ready` 114-129, `UpdateUnderwaterLook()` 282-365,
the last being 84 lines and the largest method in the file). But submersion also changes camera
*behaviour*, not just appearance: `MinPitchInWater` / `MaxPitchInWater` (8-9) and `SwimCameraLift`
feed the shared clamp in `_UnhandledInput`. Extracting water as a visual node leaves it either
owning camera behaviour or publishing a submersion signal the camera already effectively has via
`_submersion`. Recorded as intentional coupling, not accidental tidiness.

## Consequence
If the look data becomes a shared `.tres`, this is the same work as Session 4's
`## Water Look Ownership`, not a separate task. Doing the split first would mean doing it twice.
Smaller target if anything moves: one file for underwater look and submersion, with
`CameraController` retaining the pitch clamp and the lift.

# Session 6 — underwater haze colour now matches the surface (todo unaffected)

*Documents work done earlier the same day, before Sessions 4 and 5. Numbered on append per the
worklog convention, so it sits out of chronological order on purpose.*

## Where
`Scripts/Player/CameraController.cs` — the environment handover in `_Ready`.

## Symptom
The submerged view read as paler and lighter than the water surface directly above it, even though
`UnderwaterTint` and the surface's `shallow_color` held identical components.

## Cause
Godot multiplies fog colour by fog energy, so the tint was being rendered at
`(0.09, 0.31, 0.45) * 1.6 = (0.144, 0.496, 0.72)`. The *hue* was always correct, which is exactly
why the symptom was "too pale" rather than "wrong colour" and why matching the numbers by eye had
not converged. Confirmed against the Environment class reference that `fog_light_energy` is a
brightness multiplier.

## Fix
Divide at the handover rather than hardcoding a pre-divided literal, so the two cannot drift apart
the next time energy is touched:
```csharp
_underwaterEnv.FogLightColor = UnderwaterTint / Mathf.Max(UnderwaterFogEnergy, 0.01f);
```
`UnderwaterFogEnergy` is now a pure brightness knob layered over the surface colour, and the
"matches the surface" invariant holds by construction rather than by arithmetic.

## Verification
`tools/test_underwater_look.gd` — the assertion changed from raw `fog_light_color` equality to
checking the *rendered* colour, `fog_light_color * fog_light_energy == UnderwaterTint`, since the raw
value is now deliberately a fraction of the tint. 29 checks, 0 failures; rendered haze measures
`(0.09, 0.31, 0.45)` against the surface's `(0.09, 0.31, 0.45)`. Build clean, 0 warnings.

## Two open problems found while verifying, neither fixed

**1. `tools/test_underwater_filter.gd` fade checks are frame-timing flaky.** Three runs of
identical, already-built code produced 1, then 3, then 2 failures. `filter fades through
intermediate values` sampled 0 samples on one run and 2 on the next, and `filter is off at the
waterline` flipped between 1.000 and 0.000 at 0.002 m. The assertions sample a `_Process`-smoothed
value at a fixed frame count, so they race the fade. Needs a settle-based assertion rather than a
per-frame sample, independent of any fog tuning.

**2. The density comment contradicted the code. Now rewritten neutrally.**
`CameraController.cs:22-26` stated the density "It has to be denser than the air haze, not lighter" and
is "blended up from the world's own value by the tint". Both were false. The values are 0.06 and 0.4
against a world aerial fog of 0.45, so the underwater haze is *lighter* than the air haze, not denser;
and the "blended up from the world's value" approach is recorded as tried and rejected in
`CURRENT_TODO.md` precisely because it hazes the whole above-water look, with the density being
`full * _submersion` instead.

Rewritten rather than deleted, at the user's direction, to state what the code does, record the
0.9/2.2 -> 0.06/0.4 retune, and flag the rationale as unconfirmed instead of asserting a new one. The
original argument — that the haze had to out-density the air fog or the seafloor stops looking
submerged — is preserved as an open question rather than quietly dropped, because whether the
gentler pair is the intended look is not a wording problem and is still unanswered. The same
correction was applied to the `CURRENT_TODO.md` Camera -> tint item, which claimed "They are now 0.9
and 2.2" while the code said otherwise. Session 3's entry below is left as written: it is an
accurate record of what was true on the day, and the worklogs are append-only.
