## Added: the project had no collision layers

**Where:** `Scripts/Core/CollisionLayers.cs` (new), `project.godot:30-34`, `Scenes/Player.tscn:21-22`,
`Scenes/World.tscn:117-118`, `Scenes/TestWorld.tscn:21-22`, `Scripts/Player/PlayerController.cs:538`,
`Scripts/Player/CameraController.cs:355`.

**Symptom:** nothing, yet. This is a prerequisite, not a fix. Every object sat on the default
layer 1 with a default mask, so every raycast hit everything:

- `PlayerController.GroundHeightUnder` excluded only its own body and then reported whatever else
  the ray met as "the floor". With a second player present, that second player's capsule is a
  candidate for the floor.
- `CameraController.AvoidGeometry` had the same shape — intended to pull the boom in for a wall,
  equally willing to pull it in for another player walking past.
- A skillshot could not be written at all: with the player and the terrain on one layer there is
  no query that returns a player without also returning rock.

**Cause:** layers were never assigned. Godot defaults everything to layer 1 / mask 1, and two
objects both defaulting to 1 do match each other, so the arrangement looked intentional and
worked for exactly one player.

**Fix:** four named layers, with the roles and the two load-bearing masks documented in one
place rather than as literals at each site — the same reason the animation clip names live in
`AnimationIds`. `World` (1), `PlayerBody` (2), `PlayerHitbox` (4, reserved), `Projectile`
(8, reserved). The capsule masks `World` only, so the skeleton-derived combat shapes that
`PlayerHitbox` is reserved for cannot block movement. Names are mirrored into
`project.godot`'s `[layer_names]` so the editor dropdown shows the same four.

Terrain3D needs no change: its collision is generated by the addon, the addon never touches
`collision_layer`, so that collision is already on layer 1 and layer 1 is now `World`. Verified
rather than assumed — `grep -rn 'collision_layer' addons/terrain_3d/` returns nothing.

**The trap worth writing down:** `WaterZone` finds the player with `GetOverlappingBodies()`,
which matches the zone's `collision_mask` against the capsule's `collision_layer`. Those two
numbers live in two different scene files. They matched before by accident (both 1), so changing
one without the other turns water entry off with no error and no warning — and it presents as a
locomotion regression rather than a config mistake. They are edited together here, and
`tools/test_collision_layers.gd` now asserts the pairing at runtime so the next person to split
them finds out from a test.

## Added: a test runner

**Where:** `tools/run_tests.sh` (new).

**Why:** the suite was being run by hand one script at a time, and "did it pass" was being read
by eye from a wall of mipmap warnings — Terrain3D emits five of them on every single run, so the
actual verdict is buried. `test_spawn` and `test_collision` are diagnostics with nothing to
assert and print no verdict at all; that is correct behaviour and previously looked like a broken

**Note for whoever runs it next:** a test can die with SIGSEGV on the *first* run after a
`dotnet build`, before Godot has warmed its import and shader cache. That is a cold-cache
artifact, not a flake in the test — `test_wade` was run six times after the cache warmed and
passed six times. Re-run before believing a CRASH.

## Fixed: falling off a cliff fell at 3 m/s, in swim simulation, and never landed

**Where:** `Scripts/Core/LocomotionState.cs` — the `Mode` ternary and the `IsPlunging` latch.
**Symptom:** reported as "max fall speed is now low again" plus "acts very strange, sometimes not
moving, then jerks to another position" when holding jump or a movement key after leaving a high
area. Two distinct faults, one cause.

**Cause:** `InWater` means *inside the WaterZone box*, and that box covers the entire map
including ~40 m of open air above the island. Two places treated zone membership as proof of water
and so swallowed every body falling through air over dry land:

1. The `Mode` ternary ended in `: LocomotionMode.EnteringWater` as its final fallback. Every
   in-zone body that was not underwater, not at swim depth, not wading and not plunging — which
   is precisely a body falling through open air — resolved to `EnteringWater`, routing it to
   `SimulateSwim`. The comment directly above already said the ordering was "the eye wins
   outright, then submersion, then the ground"; the branch was not the ground.
2. `IsPlunging` armed on `VerticalVelocity < -PlungeSpeed` alone, deliberately independent of
   depth for good reason (a high fall reaches deep submersion while still doing 10+ m/s, and the
   swim pose was replacing the fall pose too early). But independent of depth is not independent
   of water, so any fall on the island that outran `PlungeSpeed` latched a plunge with 85 m of
   dry rock underneath — and `EnteringWater` again.

In `SimulateSwim` the dive charge asks only whether a key is down and the camera is pitched past
`DiveLookDownDeg`; it never checks for nearby water. After `DiveDelay` it clamps `_verticalVelocity`
to `-SwimSpeed`, and horizontal movement runs at `SurfaceSwimSpeed` instead of `MoveSpeed`.

**Measured** on a 45 m drop off the island, W held, camera 45 degrees down
(`tools/probe_openair_input.gd`):

| | ticks | fastest | braked | peak horizontal | modes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no input | 162 | -27.30 m/s | 0 | 0.00 | `EnteringWater` ×162 |
| W + camera down | 395 (timeout) | **-3.00 m/s** | 17 | **5.00 m/s** | `EnteringWater` ×395 |

The second drop never landed — pinned at 3 m/s it drifted out over the sea, which is the
"resets in water" part of the report.

**Fix:** hoist `waterDepth = probe.WaterSurfaceY - groundY` above the plunge latch and gate it on
`overWater = waterDepth > 0`, meaning there is genuinely water standing on the ground below. No
water below means no plunge. The mode ternary's final branch becomes the ground it was documented
to be: `probe.OnFloor ? Grounded : Airborne`. This also subsumes the earlier two-sided `shallow`
test, which now reads `overWater && waterDepth < WadeDepth`.

**Verified:** `dotnet build` 0 warnings/0 errors. `tools/probe_openair_input.gd` now reports
`Airborne` for 100% of both drops, -27.30 and -27.34 m/s, 0 braked ticks. Full suite 13 pass,
with `test_plunge`, `test_plunge_hold`, `test_swim_entry`, `test_swim_slope`, `test_ledge_wade`
and `test_wade` all still green — gating the plunge did not cost real water entry its pose.

## Added: regression test for the open-air fall

## Open question: there is no air control

`SimulateGround` gates all horizontal acceleration behind `if (groundedNow)`
(`Scripts/Player/PlayerController.cs:396`), so holding a movement key mid-air does nothing until
the body lands and then accelerates to full speed in one frame. That is a second, independent
cause of the "not moving, then jerks to another position" half of the report, and it is *not* a

lists "Air Dash" as planned work. Worth deciding explicitly before it reads as a bug again.

The glyph data model was complete enough to author against and nothing consumed it (see

the full supporting stack, and specified the scaling curve in session:

- points ≥ 1: one mid-air jump at half the normal jump's height, steering the arc slightly
  toward the held direction
- every 10 points: +1 mid-air jump (11 → third, 21 → fourth, ... 91 → eleventh; at the pool,
  ten). Each starts at half the normal jump and reaches twice it over its decade, then caps
- more points spend less mana, linearly, flooring at 0
- the chain resets on landing

### The three new layers

`GlyphLoadout` is the player-owned allocation the design doc called for: entries (GlyphData +
points) keyed by resource path against the shared 100-point pool, with `TrySetPoints` refusing
a negative count or an allocation that would overflow the pool. Points live here, never on
`GlyphData` — a `.tres` is one object, so points stored on it would be the same for every
player. A glyph built in code has no resource path, so `SameGlyph` falls back to instance
identity; two instances of the same `.tres` are the same glyph, which is also what keeps a
duplicated resource from masquerading as a second glyph.

`PlayerResources` is the payer `GlyphCost` never had: mana/health/stamina pools with the slow
passive regen that runs at all times, `CanSpend`/`TrySpend` refusing a cost the pool cannot
pay and refusing negative amounts (a cost cannot become a refund by accident). Health and
stamina have no spender yet; the pools are built together so a cost entry's resource is enough
to reach one when their glyphs arrive.

`PlayerTechniques` is the technique→locomotion wiring: `TryAirJump(airJumpsUsed,
normalJumpHeight)` is the atomic check-pay-compute — trigger, activation threshold, jump
budget, cost, spend, height — returning the height or -1 when nothing fired. It returns a
float rather than taking an `out` parameter so the GDScript tests call the exact same path
the body does. The jump COUNT is the glyph's own `JumpCount` stat (base 1, `Step` scaling,
Value 1 per StepSize 10 points), which reads exactly the number of mid-air jumps the points
buy; the per-jump HEIGHT depends on which jump you are on as well as the point count, so it
cannot be a pure scaling entry and lives in `AirJumpHeightAt`, relative to the body's normal
jump. The linear ramp inside each decade is a judgement call the design doc does not settle.

### The controller hook

`SimulateGround` (PlayerController.cs:427-449) gained the air-jump branch beside the ground
jump: the counter resets on any grounded frame (which is what "not recastable until hit
ground" becomes for a technique that buys several jumps — you chain until the points run out
of them), and the technique fires on the same press-not-hold edge the ground jump does. The
air jump also redirects: `RedirectAirJump` swings the horizontal velocity toward the held
input (`AirJumpRedirect` = 0.5 of the way through the angle) while keeping its speed — a
redirect of the existing arc, not air control from a standstill, which stays the open feel
question from Session 2. The technique deliberately rides the existing Space-key read like
the rest of the controller; action bindings land with the input map todo.

### The glyph itself

`Resources/Glyphs/DoubleJump.tres` is the first authored `.tres`: Technique, Trigger
Airborne, School Flowing, elements Zephyr + Anima (wind + body, non-damaging, per
GlyphDecisions.md "Element principles (decided)" — the todo only said Zephyr), Mana 10 up
front with -0.5 per point past activation flooring at 0, `JumpCount` base 1 with the Step
scaling. `Scenes/Player.tscn` wires the loadout (5 points, the debug allocation until the
rune setup menu exists), the pools and the technique node. The `TechniqueGlyphData` cost
comment was corrected: it claimed techniques are usually stamina; double jump pays mana by
decision.

## The trap worth writing down: C# exported node references do not resolve from a hand-written .tscn

`[Export] public PlayerTechniques Techniques;` serialized as `Techniques = NodePath("Techniques")`
did not resolve under 4.7: the property stayed null with no error and no warning, and the
technique silently never fired — the first test run showed the ground jump working and the
air jump dead. `tools/probe_techniques.gd` found it: resource exports
(`X = ExtResource(...)`) resolve fine, node-typed exports do not. The fix is the house
pattern: named path constants + `GetNodeOrNull<T>` resolved in `_Ready` (PlayerRig.cs:25-31,
76-81), which the probe confirmed. Recorded in `GlyphDecisions.md` code notes so the next
exported node reference does not rediscover it by a dead feature.

## The trigger

`docs/design/README.md` and `docs/README.md` both define a staleness test: *if a doc still
mentions `Assets/`, URP, or `Tools > Terrain`, it is stale.* Running that test across `docs/design/`
found the Animations section of `PlayerDecisions.md` had never been migrated. It described a Unity
`.controller` file and five `*Setup.cs` editor tools (`DirectionalWalkGenerator`,
`DirectionalControllerSetup`, `StickmanAnimatorSetup`, `LocomotionTransitionSetup`,
`SwimAnimationSetup`) — **none of the six exist**. The real pipeline is
`tools/build_stickman.gd` → `Scenes/Stickman.tscn` + `AnimationIds` + `LocomotionGraph`.

Worse, it was wrong about the design rather than merely dated. It claimed Walk/Run use "8-way
directional blend trees". They do not: `AnimationIds.ClipFor` picks **discrete per-direction
clips** from a flat state machine, one state per clip, and backward plays the *forward clip in
reverse* rather than using the five authored `Backward` clips. Commit `1bf85a5` ("an
AnimationTree state machine, built in code because the tree is the API") has the reasoning.

## Why the rationale was recoverable instead of invented

The obvious move was to write a plausible replacement, which is how design docs acquire
confident fiction. Instead the rationale came out of the commit body, which had preserved three
things that are not guessable and had each been **measured** rather than assumed:

- `AnimationPlayer.speed_scale` is *ignored* once a tree drives it — measured at 1.0, 2.0, 0.5 and
  -1.0, all playing at 1.0x. Per-state speed is an `AnimationNodeTimeScale` inside each state,
  addressed as `parameters/<state>/Speed/scale` (2.0 → 1.88x, 0.5 → 0.505x, -1.0 → -1.035x, 0.0
  pauses). Negative is load-bearing: backward walking *is* the forward clip reversed, and the jump
  clip is scaled to the arc it flies.
- `AnimationNodeTimeScale.scale` appears in neither `get_property_list`, nor the C# bindings, nor
  the class reference — it is registered as an AnimationTree *parameter*.
- `AnimationNodeAnimation.start_offset` is ignored unless `use_custom_timeline` is also set, and
  the custom timeline must be the clip's own length or the clip is time-warped. Measured:
  `start_offset` alone left the hand pose identical (0.308, 0.809, 0.133); with a real-length
  timeline it moved to (0.357, 0.822, 0.09).

All three are now in `PlayerDecisions.md`. Re-deriving them from memory would have reintroduced
exactly the wrongness this session removed.
